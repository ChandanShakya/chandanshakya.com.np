Here is an honest starting point: I ship production code every day, but AI writes most of it.

As I wrote in my last post, my daily workflow is prompting Claude Code and OpenCode. It is fast, it works, and the software I build at NCCS and MathUplift is real. But if you took those tools away tomorrow, how much could I actually do on my own? The honest answer is: I do not know. Even in PHP and Laravel, which I use every day, I would rate myself as mostly basic once the framework and the AI are not carrying me.

That bothers me. So I am removing AI from my learning and starting again from the beginning.

## Where the gaps are

I looked at my own work honestly, and the weak spots are clear:

- **Raw SQL** — I lean on Eloquent and let the AI write the tricky queries
- **Algorithms and data structures** — the theory I never properly learned
- **JavaScript fundamentals** — my JS is basic, and it shows
- **Testing** — I write smoke tests, not real test suites
- **Framework internals** — I use Laravel daily but could not explain half of what happens underneath

None of this stopped me from shipping. That is exactly the problem — shipping with AI help hid what I never learned.

## I have tried this before

This is not my first roadmap. I have made learning plans before — one of them had 635 checkboxes. I ticked zero. The plan was never the problem. Every time I sat down to study, reaching for the AI was easier than doing the reps myself, so the reps never happened.

This time the system is different, and the rules are stricter.

## The plan

Everything now lives in one roadmap in my notes — 17 phases, from foundations through PHP, Laravel, JavaScript, databases, algorithms, testing, security and production, ending with a capstone project. 657 checkboxes in total. It starts right at the basics: I am restarting with PHP For Beginners, episode 5, and writing every line by hand.

The real tracking lives in my notes, where every phase breaks down into individual checkboxes. I will update the numbers here as I go — a phase gets its tick only when every box inside it is done, twice over (once following along, once from memory).
The rules I set for myself:

- **No deadlines.** It takes as long as it takes. A checkbox a day is fine. A checkbox a week is fine.
- **Free resources only.** Everything I need to learn this is already free.
- **AI explains and reviews — it never writes the code I am practicing.** If I am stuck, I struggle alone for 30 minutes first. Then I can ask for an explanation, not an implementation.
- **Two passes before ticking a box.** First pass: follow along. Second pass: redo it from memory, without looking. Only then does the checkbox get ticked.
- **Weekly check-in.** Every Sunday night I report what got ticked, what blocked me, and what is next. No guilt, no targets — just an honest record.

## Why

I am not quitting AI at work — it makes me faster, and my employers get their money's worth. But I want the skill to actually be mine. I want to be able to design, build, debug and ship production software with nothing but my own head and a text editor. Using AI should be a choice I make because it is fast, not a dependence I cannot work without.
